Responsible use

Automation in enforcement goes wrong in a predictable way: a machine guess gets recorded as a finding, the finding gets quoted as a fact, and by the time anybody asks how it was reached the working has been thrown away. Most of the design decisions here exist to make that specific failure impossible.

Observation is not decision

Two records exist for every declaration, and they are never merged.

The machine record holds what optical character recognition found, with a state drawn from a fixed list: located, not detected, unreadable, ambiguous, incomplete evidence, or processing failed. It carries a confidence figure and the coordinates of the region it came from.

The officer record holds a review state: pending, confirmed, rejected, corrected or not applicable. Only an officer writes it.

A correction does not overwrite the machine reading. It writes a revision above it, so the original survives and both appear in the report. Somebody reading that report later can see that the machine read one thing, an officer read another, and which one the conclusion rests on.

Only reviewed values reach the rule engine. An unreviewed machine reading cannot produce a finding, which is enforced in the engine rather than left to the interface.

Absent evidence is never a violation

Three situations look similar on a screen and mean entirely different things:

  • The package genuinely lacks a required declaration.
  • The photograph does not show the panel where it would be.
  • The panel is visible but the text cannot be read, because of glare, blur or damage.

Only the first is a compliance failure. The second returns additional evidence required. The third returns unable to determine. Neither is reported as non-compliant, and no setting changes that.

This is measurable rather than aspirational. Running the checks on an inspection before officer review produces seven results of additional evidence required, three of unable to determine, and zero non-compliant. After review of the same evidence, ten rules apply and one non-compliant finding appears. The difference is the review, not the photograph.

Findings show their working

A finding records the rule version applied, its citation, why that version was selected for this inspection date, the input values, and the arithmetic.

Unit price is the clearest case. Where a printed unit price disagrees with price divided by quantity, the finding lists every step of the calculation, currently eight of them, with the rounding rule stated and the tolerance named. A reviewer can redo the sum on paper and get the same answer, or find the point where they disagree.

All money and quantity arithmetic uses exact decimal values, never floating point, and rounds half-up to the paise. A rounding difference in the third decimal place is not going to be the reason somebody is found non-compliant.

Why height is not read from pixels

Some requirements are about physical size. A minimum character height in millimetres is one of them.

A photograph cannot answer that question. The same letter fills more pixels from closer and fewer from further away, and without a known scale in frame there is no conversion. Software that estimated millimetres from pixels would produce a number that looked authoritative and meant nothing.

So the check refuses. Without an officer's measurement it returns additional evidence required and explains what is needed. When a measurement is supplied it is compared against the threshold together with the stated uncertainty, and a measurement whose uncertainty band crosses the threshold returns unable to determine rather than a decision that could not be defended if challenged.

Separation of duties on interpretations

A rule interpretation is an opinion about what the law requires. Treating it as configuration would let one person change the meaning of the law for everybody, quietly.

So interpretations are versioned and governed. The author of a version cannot approve it; the attempt is refused. Approval is blocked until the version has been simulated against every mandatory scenario, so nobody approves something whose behaviour they have not seen. An approved version is immutable. Changing an interpretation creates a new version and the old one stays inspectable, because a report issued last year was issued under last year's interpretation and has to remain readable on those terms.

Each version carries an effective window. A rule not yet in force on the inspection date does not apply to it, and neither does one that had already ended.

Honesty about legal authority

Every rule version has a flag recording whether its legal authority has been confirmed by somebody qualified to confirm it. For all eleven versions that ship with this software, that flag is false.

That flag is not buried. It shows on the rule, on every finding that uses it, and it prints on the report. An officer cannot use one of these interpretations without being told, in the same screen, that its citation has not been checked against the gazette.

The alternative would be to ship confident-looking citations transcribed from memory. That would be worse than useless: it would be actively misleading, and the mistake would surface at the worst possible moment, in front of somebody contesting a finding. The legal sources page sets out exactly which values are placeholders and what has to be done to each.

What the system refuses to do

  • Issue any enforcement action by itself. Every decision is recorded against a named officer with a written reason.
  • Produce an overall compliance score. A single number would hide which requirement failed and how certain anybody was about it.
  • Assert that a package is genuine. A readable barcode shows an identifier is printed on a package. It is not proof of anything else.
  • Verify the contents of a package. Net quantity needs weighing or measuring, and every report says so in its limits section.
  • Identify a person. There is no face recognition and no biometric matching of any kind.
  • Give medical, nutritional or legal advice.
  • Rank or profile manufacturers by predicted risk. Nothing here builds a watchlist.

Foreseeable misuse

Worth naming, because a design document that only lists safeguards is not being straight about the risks.

An unconfirmed interpretation used to pressure a trader. The flag warns but cannot stop an officer quoting a finding at somebody who has no way to evaluate it. The mitigation is procedural: the deploying authority confirms interpretations before officers use them in the field, and the report carries the flag into any conversation where it is produced.

Selective inspection. The system records what officers inspect. It cannot tell whether the choice of what to inspect was fair. Pattern review of inspection distribution is a supervisory task and this software does not do it.

Evidence framing. A photograph is a choice about what to include. An officer can photograph a panel in a way that makes a declaration hard to read. The quality signals will report glare and blur, which makes it visible, but they cannot establish intent.

Complainant identification. A free-text description can contain enough detail to identify who complained, particularly in a small market. The form asks for the product rather than the person, and no public endpoint returns contact details, but nothing prevents somebody writing identifying detail into a description box.

None of these is solved by code. They are the reasons a system like this belongs under supervision rather than running on its own.
