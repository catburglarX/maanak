What the law requires on a package

The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 came into force on 1 April 2011 under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009. They set out what must be declared on a pre-packaged commodity offered for retail sale. This page lists the eleven requirements this system tests, in the order you would read a label.

Read this before relying on anything below. The provision numbers shown are the ones stored against each check, and none has been confirmed against a gazette notification. Treat them as a guide to what to look for on a package, not as a citation to quote at anybody. The legal sources page explains exactly what is unverified and why, and names the values that are placeholders.

The declarations

1. Who made, packed or imported it

The name and complete address of the manufacturer, the packer, or the importer. "Complete" matters: a brand name and a city is not an address you could send a letter to.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(a) · unverified

2. What the thing actually is

The common or generic name of the commodity. A brand name is not a common name. "Riverside Gold" tells you nothing; "iodised salt" does.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(b) · unverified

3. How much is in it

The net quantity, by weight, measure or number. Net means the contents, excluding the wrapper.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(c) · unverified

4. In a unit that is permitted

The quantity has to use a standard unit. A quantity written only as "1 family pack" or "2 large" is not a declaration of net quantity.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(c), with the permitted unit lists · the lists in this system come from ordinary retail practice, not from the Second Schedule

5. When it was made

The month and year of manufacture, packing or import. A year alone is incomplete, and so is a month alone.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(d) · unverified

6. The maximum retail price

The retail sale price of the package. This is the ceiling, not a suggestion. Being charged above the printed price is the single most common reported problem, and this system treats it as the most urgent.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(e) · unverified

7. Priced in the prescribed words

The price must be expressed so that it is unambiguously inclusive of all taxes. A figure with no indication of what it includes is not a compliant declaration.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(e), with the accepted wording list · a legally acceptable phrase missing from that list would produce a wrong result

8. How to reach someone about a problem

Consumer care details: a name or designation, together with a telephone number or an email address. A postal address alone leaves you with no practical route.

Attributed to Rule 6(1)(f) · unverified

9. Where it came from, if imported

The country of origin, on an imported package. This applies to imports; a package made in India is not required to carry it by this provision.

Attributed to Rule 6(1), as amended · unverified, and the amendment date has not been entered

10. A unit price that agrees with the arithmetic

Where a unit sale price is printed, it has to be consistent with the retail sale price divided by the net quantity. This is the one requirement you can check yourself with a calculator: if a 1 kg pack is priced at ₹24.00, a printed unit price of ₹5.00 per 100 g is wrong, because the arithmetic gives ₹2.40.

Attributed to Rule 6 read with Rule 2 · the reference quantity and rounding tolerance in this system are engineering choices and are printed on every finding

11. Printed large enough to read

Declarations must meet a minimum character height. The prescribed minimum varies with the area of the principal display panel, so a large pack must print larger than a small one.

Attributed to Rule 9 · this system holds a single placeholder height and refuses to decide from a photograph. Height in millimetres cannot be derived from an image without a known scale, so it requires an officer's physical measurement

What to do if a package is missing one of these

A missing declaration on a package you actually hold is worth reporting. Photograph the panel where the declaration should be, including enough of the package that it is clearly the same item, and say what you could not find.

Photograph the whole panel rather than a crop. A close-up of one line cannot show that something is absent, only that it is not in the crop, and the system will return additional evidence required rather than a finding.

What this list is not

These eleven are the requirements this software tests. They are not the whole of packaging law. Food packages carry further labelling obligations under the Food Safety and Standards regulations, including ingredients, allergens and nutrition, and none of those is implemented or assessed here. Sector-specific rules apply to medicines, cosmetics, electrical goods and other categories.

For food labelling and safety, the authority is the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India at fssai.gov.in. For weights, measures and the declarations above, it is the Controller of Legal Metrology in your state, listed at consumeraffairs.gov.in.
